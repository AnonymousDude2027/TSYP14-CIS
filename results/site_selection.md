## Why the 95 % default is relaxed

The specification's default asks for 8 of 10 years at >= 95 % completeness for TMAX, TMIN and PRCP.  On the GHCN-Daily archive this is met by the European, North-American and Nordic stations in the bundle but **not** by the Tunisian, Egyptian and Saharan stations, where TMAX is reported on a 60-75 % duty cycle (TMIN is denser).  Two options existed: drop the North-African sites, or design for gaps.  The second was chosen because a false-data-injection system that only works on complete records is useless in exactly the regions where the challenge is motivated.

Design consequences, all of them exercised by the code:

* no interpolation anywhere in the detection path (SP1 flags gaps; every statistic is normalised by the effective sample size of its window);
* SP3's AR filter treats a missing day as a missing observation of a state-space model instead of discarding the p days that follow it (otherwise an AR(5) filter would keep 7 % of CARTHAGE's samples);
* the false-alarm calibration resamples the *actual* gap pattern, so periods with different data density do not silently change the alarm rate.
